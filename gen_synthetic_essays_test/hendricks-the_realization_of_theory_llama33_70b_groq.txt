Similarly, the three-part soul is presented as a theoretical model, designed to explain the nature of human psychology, rather than as a description of an objective reality. These models are employed by Socrates as a means of encouraging Glaucon and Adeimantus to think critically about the nature of justice and the ideal state, rather than as a means of imparting metaphysical doctrine.

However, later passages in the dialogue undermine this hierarchy, suggesting that the relationship between theory and practice is more complex than initially suggested.
