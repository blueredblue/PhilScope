# The Epistemic Mirage: Alvin Plantinga, Matthew Power, and the Probability of Naturalism

## Introduction

At the intersection of epistemology, philosophy of religion, and the philosophy of biology lies one of the most provocative analytical challenges to contemporary secular thought: Alvin Plantinga’s Evolutionary Argument Against Naturalism (EAAN). Plantinga’s thesis contends that the conjunction of metaphysical naturalism ($N$) and neo-Darwinian evolutionary theory ($E$) is epistemically self-defeating. 

The core of Plantinga’s argument relies on probability: given $N$ and $E$, the probability that our cognitive faculties are reliable ($R$) is either low or inscrutable. Consequently, anyone who accepts $N$ and $E$ acquires a defeater for $R$, which in turn provides a defeater for every belief they hold—including $N$ and $E$ itself.

While Plantinga’s original formulation has generated vast literature, Matthew Power has extended the EAAN by turning the lens onto probability assessments themselves. Power argues that if unguided natural selection targets behavioral survival rather than truth, it undermines not merely our general beliefs, but our very capacity to assign accurate probabilities to complex, abstract propositions like the conjunction of $N$ and $E$. 

This essay evaluates Plantinga’s EAAN and Power’s probabilistic extension. It addresses the central question: Can a defender of metaphysical naturalism and neo-Darwinian evolution rationally assign even a minimal or plausible probability to that worldview? 

By analyzing the mechanisms of unguided natural selection, evaluating the distinction between survival-oriented gut reactions and complex probabilistic reasoning, and examining whether theism is a necessary condition for justifying probability assessments, I will argue that the naturalist faces a profound and ultimately insurmountable epistemic deficit. Without a teleological framework ensuring a harmony between truth and cognitive faculties, the naturalist cannot coherently vindicate the reliability of the very reason used to justify naturalism.

## 1. Plantinga’s Evolutionary Argument Against Naturalism: Structure and Mechanics

To understand the extension offered by Matthew Power, one must first master the structural mechanics of Plantinga’s original argument, as articulated in *Warrant and Proper Function* (1993) and refined in *Warranted Christian Belief* (2000) and *Knowledge and Christian Belief* (2015). 

Plantinga defines naturalism as the view that there is no supernatural entity—no God, no immaterial soul, and no transcendent mind. Neo-Darwinian evolution ($E$) is the standard modern synthesis: organic life has evolved through unguided genetic mutation and natural selection. 

The argument does not claim that $E$ is false; rather, it targets the *conjunction* of $N$ and $E$. Plantinga’s argument proceeds as a formal probability problem:

1. **P(R/N & E) is low or inscrutable.** Given naturalism and evolution, the probability that our cognitive faculties are reliable ($R$) is low or impossible to determine.
2. **Anyone who accepts (N & E) and sees that P(R/N & E) is low or inscrutable has a defeater for R.**
3. **If one has a defeater for R, one has a defeater for any belief produced by one's cognitive faculties—including (N & E) itself.**
4. **Therefore, (N & E) is epistemically self-defeating and cannot be rationally accepted.**

The pivotal premise is the first: Why should $P(R/N \& E)$ be low? Plantinga approaches this through the philosophy of mind. Under naturalism, beliefs are epiphenomenal or supervenient upon physical states of the brain (e.g., neurophysiological structures, neural firing patterns, chemical gradients). Natural selection, however, cares exclusively about *behavior*, not *belief*. Evolution selects organisms based on adaptive, survival-conducive behaviors: fleeing predators, seeking sustenance, finding mates, and avoiding physical peril.

Behavior is caused by neurophysiological structures, but natural selection is indifferent to the semantic content of the beliefs that accompany those structures. As Plantinga famously illustrates with his thought experiments involving Paul the prehistoric hominid, behavior can be successfully driven by wildly false beliefs coupled with bizarre neurophysiological configurations. 

Imagine Paul wants to avoid a tiger. Under naturalism, his brain states cause him to run. 
* Suppose Paul's brain states produce the true belief: *That tiger wants to eat me, and running away will save my life.* (True belief, adaptive behavior).
* Alternatively, suppose Paul's brain states produce the false belief: *The tiger is a friendly creature offering a ride, and running away is a polite way of playing tag.* Concurrently, another neural property causes him to run away at high speed. (False belief, adaptive behavior).
* Or suppose Paul believes that the moon is made of green cheese, and this belief causes him to turn somersaults, which coincidentally frighten the tiger away. (False belief, adaptive behavior).

Because natural selection selects solely for adaptive *action*, and because millions of different semantic belief-contents can map onto the exact same adaptive neurophysiological structures, the probability that our belief-forming mechanisms are reliably tuned to produce *true* beliefs—rather than merely *adaptive* ones—is statistically low. 

Evolutionary mechanisms reward survival, and truth and survival are logically distinct properties. Therefore, $P(R/N \& E)$ is either low, highly equivocal, or inscrutable.

## 2. Matthew Power’s Extension: The Uncertainty of Reliable Probability Judgments

While critics of Plantinga (such as Evan Fales, Elliott Sober, and William Ramsey) have expended considerable energy attacking the claim that $P(R/N \& E)$ is low—arguing that true beliefs are generally more conducive to survival than false ones—Matthew Power shifts the battleground. 

Power’s extension focuses on a deeper, more fatal epistemological loop: **the formation of probability judgments themselves.**

Even if a naturalist attempts to assign a probability to $P(R/N \& E)$, Power argues that the naturalist's cognitive faculties must be presumed reliable *in order to make that very probability calculation*. If naturalism and evolution are true, unguided natural selection has shaped our cognitive capacities. But why should we trust our capacity to calculate abstract, counterfactual probabilities concerning deep evolutionary history and metaphysical truths?

Power contends that unguided natural selection does not merely threaten our first-order empirical beliefs; it fundamentally undermines our second-order epistemic capacity to make accurate *probability judgments*. Consider what is required to evaluate $P(R/N \& E)$:
1. We must abstract away from immediate survival pressures.
2. We must construct counterfactual scenarios regarding mind-body supervenience and evolutionary trajectories.
3. We must assess the logical relations between vast, complex sets of metaphysical and biological data.
4. We must assign numerical or comparative values to these abstract probabilities without falling prey to cognitive biases, evolutionary heuristics, or wishful thinking.

Under $N$ and $E$, our brains are biological organs evolved for reproductive fitness on the Pleistocene savanna. They are optimized for pattern recognition, social navigation, predator avoidance, and resource foraging under high-stress, low-information conditions. They are *not* truth-tracking supercomputers designed to evaluate abstract epistemological probabilities concerning the metaphysical underpinnings of reality.

When a naturalist asserts that $P(R/N \& E)$ is moderate-to-high (e.g., "Having true beliefs about our environment clearly aids survival, so evolution would naturally select for reliability"), they are making a complex probabilistic judgment. 

However, Power points out a vicious circularity: **To trust this probability assessment, the naturalist must already presuppose that their cognitive faculties are reliable ($R$).** But the very point at issue is whether $R$ can be justified under $N$ and $E$. 

If $R$ is in doubt because of the evolutionary origins of our cognitive faculties, then any probability judgment produced by those same faculties is infected with the exact same doubt. The naturalist cannot use their reason to validate the reliability of their reason without begging the question. 

Thus, Power extends Plantinga’s argument by showing that the uncertainty is not merely about whether $R$ is low; it is about the *inability of the naturalist to make any rational, warranted probability assignments* concerning their own epistemic competence. The probability judgments of the naturalist become groundless.

## 3. Can a Naturalist Rationally Assign a Plausible Probability to Naturalism and Evolution?

We must now confront the core question: Can a defender of metaphysical naturalism and neo-Darwinian evolution rationally assign even a minimal or plausible probability to that worldview? 

To answer this, we must examine the naturalist’s standard line of defense: **Evolutionary Epistemology and the Pragmatic Defense of Truth.**

### The Naturalist Defense: Truth-Trackers as Survival-Maximizers
The most common naturalist response to Plantinga and Power is rooted in the evolutionary connection between truth and survival. Denys DeSchryver, Michael Huemer, and others argue that true beliefs are instrumentally superior to false beliefs in navigating a complex environment. 

The argument goes like this: Organisms that possess accurate representations of their environment—where predators are, where water is located, what plants are poisonous—are demonstrably more likely to survive and reproduce than organisms burdened with systematic delusions. Therefore, natural selection acts as a filter that actively favors truth-tracking cognitive faculties. While evolution does not care about abstract truths (like quantum mechanics or calculus), it cares profoundly about empirical, middle-sized-dry-goods truths. Thus, $P(R/N \& E)$ is not low; it is reasonably high.

### The Evaluative Critique: The Granularity Problem
Does this naturalist defense succeed? When subjected to Power’s probabilistic critique, the "truth-tracker" defense reveals a fatal equivocation between *pragmatic adaptive utility* and *epistemic reliability*.

1. **Empirical vs. Metaphysical Reliability:** Even if we grant that evolution favors true beliefs regarding immediate, local, practical matters (e.g., *There is a cliff over there*), it does not follow that evolution favors reliability regarding abstract, non-practical, theoretical domains (e.g., *Metaphysical naturalism is true*, or *Neo-Darwinian evolution is unguided*). Our cognitive faculties evolved to solve problems of local survival, not to comprehend the ultimate fabric of reality.
2. **The Problem of Inscrutability:** If natural selection only guarantees local, pragmatic utility, then the probability of our faculties being reliable for *metaphysical theorizing* is completely unknown—or inscrutable. 

When a naturalist attempts to assign a probability to the conjunction of $N$ and $E$, they are operating far outside the adaptive scope of Pleistocene selection pressures. 

If $P(R/N \& E)$ is inscrutable, the naturalist cannot rationally assign a high—or even a plausible minimal—probability to $N$ and $E$. To assign a probability of, say, 0.7 to $N$ and $E$ requires a background of reliable, calibrated probabilistic reasoning. But if the reliability of that reasoning is precisely what is undermined by $N$ and $E$, the assignment is arbitrary. It is a product of unguided neurochemical firings, not rational assessment. 

Therefore, the naturalist cannot rationally assign a plausible probability to their own worldview without engaging in epistemic circularity.

## 4. The Mechanism of Unguided Natural Selection and Epistemic Destabilization

To fully appreciate why unguided natural selection destabilizes epistemic capacity, we must examine the mechanics of natural selection through an information-theoretic lens.

Natural selection operates via differential reproductive success based on phenotypic traits. In a purely naturalistic universe, these phenotypes are generated by blind physical processes: genetic mutations, environmental pressures, genetic drift, and historical contingencies. There is no intentional design, no teleology, and no guiding hand ensuring that the end-product of evolution will be a creature capable of grasping objective truth.

### The Disconnect Between Adaptation and Epistemic Warrant
Epistemic warrant requires that beliefs be produced by cognitive faculties functioning properly in a cognitive environment appropriate for those faculties, according to a design plan successfully aimed at truth. Under $N$ and $E$, the "design plan" is replaced by a historical narrative of trial-and-error elimination. 

Consider what happens to the concept of *warrant* under this paradigm:
* **The Teleological Void:** Without a teleological architect (God), there is no objective standard of "proper function" for the mind, only functional adaptation for survival. A brain state that produces a false belief while successfully driving adaptive behavior is not "malfunctioning" in an evolutionary sense; it is performing its biological function swimmingly.
* **The Epistemic Parasitism of Naturalism:** Naturalists frequently rely on science—specifically evolutionary biology and neuroscience—to justify their worldview. Yet, as Plantinga notes, naturalism and evolution are locked in a destructive embrace. If evolution is unguided, it undercuts the reliability of the very scientific instruments and rational intuitions used to discover evolution. 

### The Collapse of Second-Order Warrant
Matthew Power’s contribution shines brightest here. It is one thing to argue that first-order beliefs (e.g., *There is a bear*) might occasionally be right despite false underlying semantics; it is quite another to evaluate **second-order probability judgments** about the epistemic trustworthiness of one's entire cognitive apparatus.

To make an accurate probability judgment about $P(R/N \& E)$, one must possess meta-cognitive insight into the genesis, scope, and limitations of one's own reasoning faculties. 

However, if unguided natural selection produced our meta-cognitive faculties, why should we trust them to accurately assess their own reliability? 

Evolutionary psychology reveals that human reasoning is riddled with systematic biases designed for coalition-building, status-seeking, and error-management (e.g., the hyperactive agency detection device, confirmation bias, the availability heuristic). We evolved to win arguments and secure social standing within small hunter-gatherer bands, not to achieve objective, God’s-eye-view metaphysical certainty.

When a naturalist steps back, surveys the vast expanse of evolutionary history, and calmly declares, "I assign a high probability to the proposition that my brain, formed by blind natural selection, reliably tracks deep metaphysical truths," they are exhibiting a profound form of epistemic hubris. 

The mechanism of unguided natural selection does not merely render $P(R/N \& E)$ low; it strips the naturalist of the epistemic capacity to make any justified probability judgment on the matter whatsoever.

## 5. The Objection: Survival-Oriented Gut Reactions vs. Complex Calculations

A formidable objection raised by defenders of naturalism targets the jump from basic survival mechanisms to complex, abstract calculations. 

### The Objection
Critics argue that Plantinga and Power commit a category mistake. They treat complex, abstract scientific and philosophical reasoning as if it were governed by the same crude, reflexive "gut reactions" that governed Pleistocene survival. 

The objector argues: 
> *Sure, our ancestors relied on gut reactions to avoid predators. But human beings developed language, writing, mathematics, peer review, and formal logic. These cultural and institutional technologies transcend raw biological evolution. Complex probability calculations are not raw gut reactions; they are the result of rigorous, cumulative, self-correcting intellectual frameworks (science and philosophy) that actively filter out evolutionary biases.*

Therefore, the objector claims, pointing to primitive survival mechanisms is irrelevant when evaluating the high-level, sophisticated probability assessments made by modern naturalists.

### The Response: The Epistemic Inheritance Problem
This objection is sophisticated, but it fails to escape the foundational snare. Why? Because **cultural technologies, formal logic, and scientific peer review are themselves executed by biological brains.**

1. **The Biological Substrate:** No matter how sophisticated scientific instruments, peer-review processes, or mathematical formalisms become, they must ultimately be processed, interpreted, and validated by individual human minds. The outputs of supercomputers, particle accelerators, and statistical models are translated into conscious neural representations inside a biological skull. 
2. **Inherited Unreliability:** If the baseline cognitive faculties produced by unguided evolution are fundamentally untrustworthy or structurally disconnected from truth-tracking (as Plantinga argues), then stacking sophisticated cultural technologies on top of them does not solve the problem—it merely compounds it. A skyscraper built on a swamp does not become stable simply by adding more floors; each additional floor increases the structural strain on a compromised foundation.
3. **The Logical Circularity of Science:** The naturalist uses scientific reasoning to establish evolution, and evolution to explain the brain, and the brain to justify scientific reasoning. This is a grand, sweeping circle. Under $N$ and $E$, there is no external, independent anchor to guarantee that this closed loop of reasoning corresponds to objective reality. 

Complex calculations are simply chains of basic cognitive steps (memory retrieval, inference, pattern recognition, analogical reasoning). If each link in the chain is forged by a blind, non-truth-directed evolutionary process, the long chain of reasoning is no more reliable than its weakest, most biologically contingent link. 

Thus, the distinction between "survival-oriented gut reactions" and "complex calculations" collapses: both are products of the same unguided evolutionary history.

## 6. Teleology, God, and Probability: Is Theism a Necessary Condition for Justifying Probability Assessments?

If metaphysical naturalism and neo-Darwinian evolution undermine the rationality of probability assessments, what is the alternative? Does theism provide a superior epistemic grounding? 

This brings us to the final analytical stage of our inquiry: **Theistic Epistemology and the Imago Dei.**

### The Theistic Solution
In Plantinga’s epistemological framework, theism provides a harmonious alignment between the human mind and the structure of reality—what philosophers call *cognitive fit*. 

Under theistic personalism (or classical theism), God created human beings in His image (*Imago Dei*). This entails that:
1. God possesses a supreme intellect and intended for human beings to know truth, particularly regarding moral, spiritual, and empirical realities.
2. God designed our cognitive faculties with a "design plan" successfully aimed at producing true beliefs when functioning properly in an appropriate environment.
3. There is a guaranteed correspondence between the rational structures of the human mind and the rational structures of the created universe (a point famously argued by thinkers like René Descartes and supported by modern philosophers of science like John Lennox and Alvin Plantinga).

Given theism ($T$), the probability that our cognitive faculties are reliable ($R$) is exceptionally high ($P(R/T)$ is close to 1). God has no reason to create minds systematically disposed toward massive, systemic error regarding fundamental reality. 

### Is Theism Necessary for Justifying Probability Assessments?
This raises the crucial question: Is theism a *necessary condition* for justifying probability assessments? 

Critics of Plantinga often charge him with committing a false dilemma: *Either unguided evolution (which destroys reason) or theism (which saves it).* Couldn’t a non-theistic naturalist appeal to emergentism, Platonism, or cosmic bootstrapping to ground the reliability of reason?

Let us evaluate these non-theistic alternatives:
* **Emergentism:** The view that consciousness and rationality emerge naturally from complex neurobiological systems. While emergentism describes *how* complexity arises, it does not explain *why* that complexity should be aligned with objective truth rather than adaptive illusion. Why should physical complexity yield truth-tracking rather than sophisticated fiction? Emergentism names the mystery; it does not solve it.
* **Platonic Realism about Reason:** Some naturalists or secular philosophers appeal to abstract logical and mathematical structures that exist independently of God. However, in a purely naturalistic universe (where everything is ultimately reducible to physical matter-energy), the ontological status of abstract Platonic entities is deeply problematic. If abstract norms of rationality exist independently of minds, how do biological brains access them? Without a bridge between the physical brain and abstract rational norms, naturalism struggles to account for epistemic contact.

### The Ultimate Epistemic Burden
When we evaluate the totality of the epistemic landscape, Matthew Power’s extension drives the debate to its starkest conclusion: **Probability assignments require normative foundations.**

To assign a probability to a complex proposition, one must assume standards of rationality, coherence, and evidence that transcend mere biological conditioning. 
* Under **naturalism**, these standards are accidental byproducts of Pleistocene survival pressures—epistemic epiphenomena with no guaranteed tether to objective truth. 
* Under **theism**, these standards are reflections of a transcendent, rational Mind that ground the intelligibility of both the universe and human consciousness.

Therefore, while a naturalist can psychologically *feel* confident in assigning a probability to their worldview, they cannot *rationally justify* that assignment without presupposing the very reliability that their worldview renders improbable. 

Theism is not merely an optional add-on; it functions as a necessary conceptual condition for securing the epistemic warrant required to make coherent, justified probability judgments in the first place.

## Conclusion

The evolutionary argument against naturalism, as originally formulated by Alvin Plantinga and rigorously extended by Matthew Power, inflicts a devastating blow upon the epistemic foundations of metaphysical naturalism. 

Can a defender of metaphysical naturalism and neo-Darwinian evolution rationally assign even a minimal or plausible probability to that worldview? 

The analysis conducted in this essay demonstrates that they cannot. 
* **First**, unguided natural selection targets adaptive behavior, not semantic truth, rendering the probability of cognitive reliability ($R$) low or inscrutable under $N$ and $E$. 
* **Second**, Power’s extension reveals that this deficit is recursive: it infects not only first-order beliefs, but the very second-order capacity to make justified, calibrated probability judgments about one's own epistemic competence. 
* **Third**, attempts to rescue naturalism by appealing to "truth-trackers" or "complex calculations" fail because all cultural technologies, scientific methods, and logical inferences must ultimately be executed by biological brains forged in an unguided evolutionary crucible.

The naturalist is caught in a trap of epistemic circularity: to validate their reason, they must use the reason whose reliability is called into question by their own worldview. 

Ultimately, the tension can only be resolved by recognizing that probabilistic reasoning, rationality, and truth require a teleological anchor. In the absence of a transcendent Mind to guarantee the harmony between our cognitive faculties and the structure of reality, naturalism collapses into self-referential incoherence. 

The naturalist’s probability assessment is not a rational conclusion; it is an epistemic mirage—generated by a brain that evolved to survive, not to comprehend the ultimate truth of its own existence.
