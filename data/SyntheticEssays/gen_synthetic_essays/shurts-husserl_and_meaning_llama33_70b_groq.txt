In the realm of philosophical inquiry, the concept of textual interpretation has long been a subject of debate, with various theories attempting to navigate the complexities of meaning and understanding. One philosopher who has significantly contributed to this discussion is Edmund Husserl, through his concept of the noema. This essay aims to evaluate how Husserl's noema secures a theory of textual interpretation that avoids radical relativism, by examining the mechanism of intentionality and the tension between locating meaning within the text versus projecting meaning through subjective acts of the reader.

To begin with, it is essential to understand Husserl's concept of the noema. In his work, "Ideas Pertaining to a Pure Phenomenology and to a Phenomenological Philosophy," Husserl introduces the noema as the object of thought, which is distinct from the physical object itself (Husserl, 1913). The noema is the intentional object, which is the object as it is experienced and understood by the consciousness. This concept is central to Husserl's phenomenological approach, as it allows for a detailed analysis of the structures of conscious experience.

The mechanism of intentionality, as described by Husserl, is the process by which acts of consciousness are directed toward an intentional object, producing noemata. These noemata comprise both a shared core-stratum of Sinn (sense) and individualizing thetic characters of Gegebenheitsweise (manner of givenness) (Husserl, 1913). The shared core-stratum of Sinn refers to the common meaning or sense that is shared among different experiences of the same object. This core-stratum is the objective aspect of the noema, which is independent of individual subjective experiences. On the other hand, the individualizing thetic characters of Gegebenheitsweise refer to the unique manner in which the object is given to each individual consciousness. This aspect of the noema is subjective, as it is shaped by the individual's experiences, perspectives, and biases.

The tension between locating meaning strictly within the text versus projecting meaning entirely through the subjective acts of the reader is a central issue in textual interpretation. Radical relativism, which argues that meaning is entirely subjective and relative to individual perspectives, is a position that Husserl's concept of the noema seeks to avoid. According to Husserl, the noema provides a balance between the objective and subjective aspects of meaning, allowing for a shared understanding of the text while still acknowledging the individualizing characters of Gegebenheitsweise.

The noematic relationship between consciousness and the text is crucial in securing a theory of textual interpretation that avoids radical relativism. By recognizing that the noema is both a shared and individualized object, Husserl's theory acknowledges that meaning is not solely located within the text, nor is it entirely projected by the subjective acts of the reader. Instead, the meaning of the text emerges from the intentional relationship between the consciousness and the text, which is mediated by the noema. This relationship allows for a shared understanding of the text, while still permitting individual interpretations and perspectives.

One of the key advantages of Husserl's noema is that it provides a framework for understanding how different interpretations of a text can be valid and meaningful, without succumbing to radical relativism. By recognizing that the noema comprises both a shared core-stratum of Sinn and individualizing thetic characters of Gegebenheitsweise, Husserl's theory acknowledges that different readers can have different, yet valid, interpretations of the same text. This is because each reader's intentional experience of the text is shaped by their unique experiences, perspectives, and biases, which are reflected in the individualizing characters of Gegebenheitsweise.

However, this does not mean that any interpretation is equally valid, or that the meaning of the text is entirely subjective. The shared core-stratum of Sinn provides a common ground for understanding, which serves as a constraint on the range of possible interpretations. This constraint ensures that interpretations are not entirely arbitrary, but are instead grounded in the objective aspects of the noema. In other words, the shared core-stratum of Sinn provides a foundation for understanding, which is independent of individual subjective experiences.
