**Title: TheRegress of Grounds: Why Non-Necessitarians Cannot Escape the Ontology of Totality**

**Introduction**

The central ambition of truthmaker theory is to provide a robust ontological correspondence between what is true and what exists. A proposition is true, on this view, not merely because it coheres with other propositions or possesses some deflationary property, but because there is an entity—a truthmaker—in the world that makes it true. Within this broad framework, a profound schism emerges regarding the modal force of this relation. *Truthmaker necessitarianism*, most forcefully articulated by David Armstrong, holds that if an entity *T* is a truthmaker for a proposition *P*, then the existence of *T* *necessitates* the truth of *P*: in every possible world where *T* exists, *P* is true. The relation is one of entailment, leaving no "slack" between the world and the truth. In stark contrast, *non-necessitarianism* (or contingent truthmaking) denies this modal rigidity, allowing that a truthmaker can exist while the proposition it makes true is false in some other possible world.

This dialectical tension is most acute when we consider the truthmakers for universal generalizations and negative existentials. Armstrong famously argued that to make "All Fs are Gs" true, one cannot merely point to the individual Fs that are Gs, since this collection does not rule out the existence of a non-G F in another possible world. Instead, he posited a *totality fact*—a state of affairs that includes all the Fs and their being Gs, plus a relation of totality that excludes any other F. Similarly, for "There are no unicorns," he posited a totality fact about the entire world, excluding unicorns. Non-necessitarians reject this, arguing that the actual instances suffice, or that the world's being a certain way is enough, even if it does not necessitate the truth.

However, a deeper, more insidious problem lurks beneath this surface dispute. It concerns the truthmakers of *higher-order truthmaking facts*. If a truthmaker *T* makes *P* true, then the proposition " *T* makes *P* true" is itself a truth. What makes *this* truth true? If the truthmaking relation is contingent, then the fact that *T* makes *P* true is a contingent fact, requiring its own truthmaker. This threatens an infinite regress of contingent truthmakers. In this essay, I will evaluate this dialectical tension and argue that non-necessitarians cannot adequately account for these higher-order truthmaking facts. They face a fatal dilemma: either they succumb to a vicious infinite regress of contingent truthmakers, or, in attempting to halt that regress, they are forced to posit fundamental facts that necessitate the lower-level truths—thereby collapsing into an ontological framework functionally equivalent to Armstrong's necessitarian orthodoxy, complete with its totality facts.

**Section 1: Armstrong's Truthmaker Necessitarianism and the Totality Fact**

To understand the force of the non-necessitarian challenge, one must first appreciate the rigor of Armstrong's system. For Armstrong, the world is not a mere collection of particulars and properties, but a world of *states of affairs*. A state of affairs is a structured entity, a particular instantiating a universal (e.g., *a's being F*). These states of affairs are the fundamental truthmakers. The truthmaker principle is simple: for every true proposition, there exists at least one state of affairs that makes it true.

Crucially, Armstrong insists on the *necessitation* condition. He writes that the relation between a truthmaker and a truth is one of entailment: if the state of affairs exists, then the proposition must be true. This is not a mere psychological or epistemological guarantee; it is a metaphysical one. The existence of the truthmaker leaves no room for the truth to fail. This condition is what gives truthmaker theory its explanatory power. It distinguishes a genuine truthmaker from a mere correlate. If a truthmaker did not necessitate its truth, then we could imagine a world where the truthmaker exists but the proposition is false, which would sever the link between truth and reality that the theory is meant to secure.

This necessitarian commitment forces Armstrong to confront the problematic cases of universals and negatives. Consider the universal generalization "All ravens are black." The truthmaker cannot simply be the collection of individual ravens that are black—Raven1's being black, Raven2's being black, and so on. This collection, while existing in the actual world, does not necessitate the truth of the generalization. There are possible worlds where this exact collection of ravens exists (or where these particular ravens exist and are black) but where there is also an additional raven that is white. In such a world, the collection of actual black ravens exists, yet the proposition "All ravens are black" is false. Therefore, the mere conjunction of positive states of affairs cannot serve as a truthmaker for a universal generalization under the necessitation constraint.

Armstrong's solution is to posit a *totality state of affairs*. The truthmaker for "All ravens are black" is not just the collection of black ravens, but a complex state of affairs that includes this collection *and* a relation of totality that holds between the collection and the universal "raven." This totality fact states that the collection of black ravens is *all* the ravens there are. This totality fact, because it includes the exclusion of any other ravens, *necessitates* the truth of the generalization. In any world where this totality fact exists, there are no other ravens, and all existing ravens are black.

The same strategy applies to negative existentials. For "There are no unicorns," Armstrong cannot point to any positive state of affairs involving unicorns, as there are none. Instead, he posits a "totality fact" about the entire world. The truthmaker is the state of affairs that the world is *all* there is—a maximal state of affairs that includes all existing entities and a relation of totality that excludes everything else. This totality fact, by excluding unicorns, necessitates the truth that there are no unicorns. Without such a totality fact, the non-necessitarian would be left with nothing to rule out the existence of unicorns, and the negative proposition would lack a necessitating ground.

Armstrong's system, therefore, is built on a foundation of totality facts. These facts are not reducible to their constituent parts; they are genuine states of affairs with a unique structure. They are the price of admission for a necessitarian theory that hopes to account for all truths.

**Section 2: The Non-Necessitarian Alternative and the Problem of Slack**

Non-necessitarians reject this entire edifice. They argue that the necessitation condition is too strong and that it forces an inflationary ontology of dubious "totality facts." Instead, they propose a weaker, contingent relation between truthmakers and truths. A truthmaker, on this view, is simply that entity in the world *in virtue of which* a proposition is true, but it does not *entail* the truth. The existence of the truthmaker is sufficient for the truth in the actual world, but it does not guarantee it across all possible worlds.

For universal generalizations, the non-necessitarian claims that the truthmaker for "All ravens are black" is simply the collection of actual ravens being black. They concede that this collection does not necessitate the generalization, because there could be other ravens. However, they argue that this is not a problem. The truth of "All ravens are black" is a truth about the *actual* world. The truthmaker—the collection of actual black ravens—is a fact about the actual world. The fact that there could have been a white raven in another possible world does not undermine the truth of the proposition in the actual world. The truthmaker merely needs to *track* the truth in the actual world, not legislate across all possible worlds.

Similarly, for negative existentials, the non-necessitarian might argue that the truthmaker for "There are no unicorns" is simply the world's being a certain way—namely, devoid of unicorns. They might point to the entire world, or to the absence of unicorn-instantiations. This absence, or this state of the world, makes the proposition true, but it does not necessitate it. There are possible worlds where the world contains unicorns, and in those worlds, the proposition is false. But in the actual world, the absence of unicorns is a fact, and it is this fact that grounds the truth.

This stance introduces what Armstrong derisively calls "truthmaker slack." If the existence of the truthmaker does not necessitate the truth, then it is unclear what metaphysical work the truthmaker is doing. If the world could be exactly as it is (with all the black ravens, and no unicorns) and yet the proposition "All ravens are black" could be false, then the truthmaker seems to be a mere shadow of the truth, not a genuine ground. The non-necessitarian responds that this is a misunderstanding of the nature of truthmaking. Truthmaking is not about modal entailment; it is about ontological grounding. The truthmaker is that entity which, in the actual world, is responsible for the truth. The fact that it might not be responsible in another world is irrelevant to its actual role.

However, this response overlooks a critical difficulty. The non-necessitarian's account of truthmaking generates a problem when we shift our attention from the first-order truths (like "All ravens are black") to the higher-order truths about the truthmaking relation itself.

**Section 3: The Regress of Higher-Order Truthmaking Facts**

Consider a simple atomic truth, *P*: "Socrates is wise." Let *T1* be the state of affairs *Socrates' being wise*. According to the non-necessitarian, *T1* makes *P* true. Now consider the higher-order proposition *Q*: " *T1* makes *P* true." This proposition is itself a truth. It is a fact about the world that there exists a truthmaking relation between *T1* and *P*. What is the truthmaker for *Q*?

For the necessitarian, this is not a problem. Because the truthmaking relation is internal and necessitated by its relata, the truthmaker for *Q* is simply *T1* itself (or the state of affairs *T1* + *P*). The existence of *T1* entails the truth of *P*, and thus entails that *T1* makes *P* true. The higher-order truth is automatically grounded by the first-order state of affairs.

But for the non-necessitarian, this path is blocked. Since *T1* does not necessitate *P*, the existence of *T1* does not entail that *T1* makes *P* true. The relation between *T1* and *P* is a contingent relation. It is a fact that *T1* and *P* are connected in this way, but this fact is not necessitated by *T1* alone. Therefore, *Q*—the proposition that *T1* makes *P* true—requires its own truthmaker, *T2*. *T2* must be a state of affairs that grounds the fact that *T1* stands in the truthmaking relation to *P*.

What could *T2* be? It cannot be *T1* itself, because *T1* does not necessitate *P*, and thus does not necessitate the truthmaking relation. It must be some further fact about the world—perhaps a fact about the laws of nature, or a fact about the specific configuration of *T1* and *P*, or a brute fact about the relation holding. Let us suppose *T2* exists and makes *Q* true. But now we have a new truth, *R*: " *T2* makes *Q* true." Since the truthmaking relation is contingent, *T2* does not necessitate *Q*. Therefore, *R* requires its own truthmaker, *T3*. And so on, *ad infinitum*.

This is a *vicious* infinite regress, not a benign one. It is vicious for several reasons. First, it is ontologically inflationary. Each step in the regress introduces a new entity ( *T2*, *T3*, *T4*, ...) into our ontology. The non-necessitarian, who sought to avoid Armstrong's bloated ontology of totality facts, ends up with an even more bloated ontology of truthmaking facts for truthmaking facts. Second, it is explanatory bankrupt. The regress never provides a fundamental ground for the original truthmaking relation. We never arrive at a point where we can say, "This is just how the world is, and this grounds the truthmaking relation." Instead, we are perpetually deferring the explanation to a higher-order truthmaker, which itself requires an explanation. The regress leaves the original truthmaking relation ungrounded, floating on an infinite chain of contingent facts.

The non-necessitarian might attempt to halt the regress by positing a primitive truthmaking relation. They might say that the fact that *T1* makes *P* true is a brute fact, a fundamental feature of reality that does not require a further truthmaker. This is a common response. However, it is deeply unsatisfying and ultimately self-defeating. If the truthmaking relation is a brute fact, then it is a fundamental entity in the world. But if it is a fundamental entity, then it exists in the world. And if it exists in the world, then the proposition "The truthmaking relation holds between *T1* and *P*" is a truth. According to the truthmaker principle (which the non-necessitarian presumably accepts, since they are engaging in truthmaker theory), this truth requires a truthmaker. If the truthmaking relation is a brute fact, then it is its own truthmaker. But this is precisely what the non-necessitarian cannot accept, because a brute fact that makes a truth true *necessitates* that truth. If the truthmaking relation is a brute fact, then the existence of that fact entails that *T1* makes *P* true. But if *T1* makes *P* true, and this is entailed by the brute fact, then the brute fact necessitates the truthmaking relation, which in turn necessitates *P*. This collapses back into necessitarianism.

Alternatively, the non-necessitarian might deny that higher-order truthmaking facts require truthmakers. They might argue that the truthmaker principle only applies to first-order truths about the world, not to truths about the truthmaking relation itself. This is a form of truthmaker deflationism. But this move is ad hoc and undermines the entire project. If the truthmaking relation is not itself grounded in the world, then it is a mysterious, unexplained primitive that floats free of ontology.
