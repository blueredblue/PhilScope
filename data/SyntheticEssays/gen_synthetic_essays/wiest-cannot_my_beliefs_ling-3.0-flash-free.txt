The problem of motivation has long stood at the center of philosophical inquiry into human action. At its core lies a deceptively simple question: what moves us to act? For centuries, the dominant answer has been rooted in the Humean tradition, which posits that reason alone is inert and that motivation requires an underlying desire. However, the rationalist tradition, most notably articulated by Thomas Nagel, challenges this orthodoxy by arguing that practical judgment and belief can generate motivation independently of preexisting desires.
