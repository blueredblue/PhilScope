# The Phenomenology of the Temporal Edge: Equivalence, Retrograde Ontology, and the Evidential Bankruptcy of Presentism

### I. Introduction: The Metaphysical Divergence and Phenomenological Convergence

The ontology of time has long been dominated by a dialectical trench warfare between Presentism—the view that only the present is real—and the static, detemporalized eternalism of the traditional Growing Block and block universe models. Yet, contemporary analytic metaphysics has begun to chart the periphery of this terrain, investigating heterodox models of temporal becoming that challenge our standard cognitive topologies. Among these, the **Diminishing Block theory** (in which the future is an infinitely dense, pre-existent continuum that is progressively annihilated or un-created into nothingness at a moving present edge) and the **Backwards Block theory** (in which time ontologically originates at a terminal future boundary and accretion occurs backward toward an expanding, yet-to-be-actualized past) represent radical inversions of standard metaphysical intuition.

The primary philosophical challenge posed by these heterodox models is not merely whether they are logically coherent, but whether they are *phenomenologically indistinguishable* from traditional dynamic ontologies. Common-sense human experience—our felt sense of passage, the asymmetry of anticipation and memory, and the raw qualitative edge of the "now"—is traditionally weaponized by presentists and growing-block theorists as empirical evidence for their respective frameworks. 

This essay evaluates the claim that alternative models of temporal becoming—specifically the Diminishing Block and Backwards Block theories—can generate subjective experiences of temporal flow entirely indistinguishable from those produced by the Growing Block model. By analyzing the structural isomorphism between a subject's internal memory hierarchy, their psychological scaling of importance, and the objective edge of temporal existence, I argue that the mechanisms of subjective passage are functionally decoupled from macro-ontological directions. 

Furthermore, I will assess whether Michael Dummett’s classic critique of backward causation undermines the internal coherence of the Backwards Block model. Finally, I will determine whether the psychological indistinguishability of these disparate temporal structures subverts the evidential weight that common-sense human experience lends to traditional presentist or growing-block ontologies, ultimately pointing toward a deflationary conclusion regarding phenomenological access to fundamental physics.

### II. Mapping the Ontological Landscape: Growing, Diminishing, and Backwards Blocks

To evaluate the phenomenological outputs of these models, we must first rigorously define their physical and metaphysical architectures.

#### The Growing Block Universe (GBU)
Popularized by C.D. Broad, the GBU posits that the past and the present are real, while the future is ontologically non-existent—a realm of pure potentiality or sheer nothingness. As time passes, the universe undergoes ontological accretion; new events, states of affairs, and slices of spacetime are continuously brought into being at the dynamic boundary known as the present. In this view, the "edge" of time is an active wavefront of creation. The amount of reality in the universe is strictly monotonic: it increases with every passing moment.

#### The Diminishing Block Universe (DBU)
The DBU inverts the GBU’s temporal asymmetry. Rather than a universe that grows from a minimal past toward an infinite future, the DBU postulates a universe whose future is already fully actualized, determinate, and infinitely vast, but which is systematically being consumed, annihilated, or un-created at a moving temporal edge. As the "now" advances, slices of reality are erased, sliding from existence into ontological oblivion. The past, in the DBU, does not accumulate; rather, it is the future that serves as the infinite reservoir from which the present carves away existence, leaving behind only the vanishing footprints of immediate retention.

#### The Backwards Block Universe (BBU)
More radical still is the Backwards Block theory. In standard models, temporal becoming—however conceived—proceeds from past to future. The BBU posits that the fundamental ontological anchor of the universe is located in what we conventionally call the *distant future* (or a terminal boundary condition), and that ontological accretion proceeds *retrograde*—building backward from the future toward the past. In this framework, what we experience as the "past" is the newly actualized edge of an expanding block whose foundational bedrock was laid down at the end of time. The causal and existential arrows are inverted: the future *makes* the past real, rather than the past *determining* the future.

### III. The Mechanics of Indistinguishability: Memory Hierarchies and Psychological Scaling

The central thesis under investigation is that DBU and BBU frameworks can produce a subjective stream of consciousness utterly indistinguishable from that of a GBU subject. To demonstrate this, we must examine the cognitive architecture of the subject embedded within these universes.

Phenomenological temporal flow—what William James famously characterized as the "specious present"—does not directly mirror fundamental physical ontology. Instead, it is a downstream product of internal neuro-cognitive data processing, specifically:
1. **The Memory Hierarchy**: The asymmetric storage, degradation, and retrieval of informational states.
2. **The Psychological Scaling of Importance**: The asymmetric valuation of prospective versus retrospective states, driven by evolutionary pressures and thermodynamic constraints.
3. **The Indexical "Now"**: The structural anchoring of subjective perspective to a localized point of computational processing.

#### The Subject in the Diminishing Block
Consider a conscious agent situated within a Diminishing Block Universe. At $t_1$, the agent possesses a rich array of memory traces. In a standard GBU, these memory traces are physical records of actualized past events. In the DBU, however, these same memory traces are structural features of a shrinking reality. As the ontological edge sweeps forward (consuming the universe from the future-side, or eating away at the block), the agent's brain states are continually reconfigured. 

Crucially, the *informational asymmetry* required for a subjective sense of flow remains intact. Even though the future is being annihilated or the past is being left behind in a dying wake, the agent’s brain retains high-fidelity informational patterns of "earlier" states (relative to the local sweep) and zero informational access to "later" states that have not yet been reached—or in the DBU, states that are about to be consumed. Because consciousness supervenes on local, instantaneous neurophysiological organization and its internal informational gradients, an agent in a DBU experiences a unidirectional accumulation of memory, an anticipation of the unknown, and a vivid "now." The fact that the macro-ontology is one of *subtraction* rather than *addition* makes no causal or functional difference to the local computational engine of the brain, provided the local entropy gradient and informational storage mechanisms remain asymmetric.

#### The Subject in the Backwards Block
The Backwards Block presents a more severe prima facie challenge to phenomenological equivalence. If the universe is building backward from the future, wouldn't a subject's psychological arrow of time point toward the past? Wouldn't they remember the future and anticipate the past?

This objection commits a category mistake by conflating *ontological genesis* with *epistemic accessibility*. In the BBU, the objective ontological anchor is at the future boundary, and existence propagates backward. However, *local physical processes*—thermodynamic gradients, metabolic reactions, and neural computation—still operate according to local laws of entropy increase. 

If entropy increases in the direction of ontological propagation (backward), then information is still stored in a gradient: states that are ontologically prior in the retrograde-building process (which we call the "future") act as the sources of thermodynamic imprint upon states built later (which we call the "past"). Consequently, a brain in a BBU will still process inputs such that complex traces are left of states "behind" the retrograde wave. 

Thus, the subject retains memories of the *past* (the newly actualized edge) and anticipates the *future* (the unactualized, yet-to-be-reached terminus of the retrograde wave). The subjective feeling of moving *forward* from past to future is preserved because the structural asymmetry of the internal brain states—the ratio of detailed past records to vague, indeterminate future expectations—remains identical to that of a GBU agent. The machinery of consciousness is blind to the metaphysical provenance of its underlying spacetime slices; it reads only the local informational gradients.

### IV. The Structural Isomorphism of the Specious Present

To formalize this indistinguishability, we can examine the structural isomorphism between the subject's internal states and the external temporal architecture. Let $\mathcal{S}$ be a conscious subject whose experiential state at any local index $n$ is a function of:
*   $\mathcal{M}_n$: The set of retained informational states (memory).
*   $\mathcal{P}_n$: The immediate perceptual content of the local boundary (the "now").
*   $\mathcal{A}_n$: The set of indeterminate or projected states (anticipation).

In a **Growing Block**, $\mathcal{M}_n$ represents actualized past spacetime points, $\mathcal{P}_n$ is the moving accretion edge, and $\mathcal{A}_n$ is non-existent ontological space.
In a **Diminishing Block**, $\mathcal{M}_n$ represents retained traces of states that have survived the consumption wave, $\mathcal{P}_n$ is the vanishing consumption edge, and $\mathcal{A}_n$ represents the vast, pre-existent future domain yet to be erased.
In a **Backwards Block**, $\mathcal{M}_n$ represents structural traces left by the retrograde wave, $\mathcal{P}_n$ is the active backward-building boundary, and $\mathcal{A}_n$ represents the unactualized past domain toward which the wave is propagating.

Phenomenologically, the conscious subject has no direct epistemic access to the ontological status of $\mathcal{A}_n$ or $\mathcal{M}_n$ beyond their current informational representation in $\mathcal{P}_n$. Whether $\mathcal{A}_n$ is "nothingness" (GBU), "infinite pre-existent future" (DBU), or "unactualized past" (BBU) makes no difference to the subjective calculus. The ratio of memory to anticipation, mediated by the psychological scaling of importance (wherein survival and agency compel us to weight immediate survival parameters heavily while treating the unexperienced domain as open or closed), yields an identical subjective phenomenology. The internal "feeling of flow" is simply the continuous updating of $\mathcal{P}_n$ via the transformation of perceptual inputs into memory traces—a computational process agnostic to whether the substrate is growing, shrinking, or building in reverse.

### V. Dummettian Objections: Backward Causation and the Coherence of the Backwards Block

Can a model like the Backwards Block truly be coherent, or does it founder upon logical and causal impossibilities? The most formidable philosophical obstacle to the BBU comes from the work of **Michael Dummett**, particularly his seminal arguments against the coherence of backward causation.

#### Dummett’s Critique of Retrograde Causal Influence
In classic essays such as "Bringing About the Past" (1964) and "Can an Effect Precede Its Cause?" (1954), Dummett investigated whether it is logically possible for an agent to perform an action now that brings about an event in the past. Dummett argued that the concept of backward causation collapses under conceptual analysis because of the asymmetric nature of *bringing about* versus *discovering*.

Dummett’s core insight rests on the conditions for agency and epistemic control. To say an agent can cause $E$ at $t_1$ by an action at $t_2$ (where $t_2 > t_1$), the agent must be able to know (or have justified belief) that $E$ has *not* already occurred, and that their action at $t_2$ is both necessary and sufficient for bringing $E$ about. However, if $E$ is in the past ($t_1$), it is already determinate, settled, and knowable via evidence. If the agent tries to "bring about" $E$ at $t_2$, but $E$ has already occurred, the agent's action is causally redundant. If $E$ has *not* occurred, then the past was not what we thought it was, which destabilizes the very notion of a fixed, knowable past. Dummett argues that the asymmetry of agency—the fact that we can alter the future but only investigate the past—is constitutive of the concept of causation itself. To sever causation from the temporal order of GBU-style accretion is to render the language of "cause" and "effect" logically incoherent.

#### Defending the Backwards Block Against Dummett
How does the Backwards Block theory weather Dummett’s critique? 

First, we must distinguish between *retrograde ontological accretion* and *backward causation within a standard framework*. Dummett’s critique targets agents attempting to send causal signals backward within a universe whose macro-time flows forward. In the BBU, however, the fundamental direction of ontological grounding is retrograde, but *local thermodynamic and epistemic arrows* may still align with the direction of the building wave, or the very definition of causation must be re-engineered.

If the BBU posits that existence propagates backward from the future, then what we call "causes" and "effects" are simply descriptions of the retrograde grounding relation. An event at $t_2$ (ontologically prior in the BBU, though later in indexical time) grounds the state at $t_1$. The subject inside the BBU does not experience backward causation in the problematic Dummettian sense; the subject experiences standard local causation because their neurobiology operates along the local entropic gradient of the retrograde wave. 

To use an analogy: imagine a computer rendering a digital simulation from the end of a movie file backward to the beginning. To the characters inside the simulation, events unfold sequentially from frame 1 to frame 100. The characters do not experience the rendering process; they only experience the internal logical consistency of the frames as they are actualized. Dummett’s critique assumes an agent trying to manipulate the rendering engine from the inside against the logical grain of time. But if the entire metaphysical architecture is retrograde, no agent is "bringing about the past" in a contradictory way; rather, the past is being *actualized* by the future in a way that respects local computational and causal consistency. 

Thus, Dummett’s critique successfully rules out *local retrocausal agency* within a forward-flowing ontology, but it does not invalidate a global retrograde ontology (the BBU) where the experiential perspective remains locally insulated from the global metaphysical direction.

### VI. Evidential Bankruptcy: Subverting the Claims of Presentism and Growing Block

We now arrive at the critical epistemological payoff of this investigation: *If DBU and BBU frameworks can generate subjective experiences of temporal flow entirely indistinguishable from those produced by the Growing Block model, what happens to the evidential weight of common-sense human experience?*

#### The Phenomenological Argument for Presentism and GBU
Presentists and Growing Block theorists have long relied on what may be called the *Phenomenological Argument from Passage*. The argument runs as follows:
1. We directly experience time as passing; we feel the dynamic flow from the past, through a privileged present, into an open or non-existent future.
2. Our introspective and phenomenological access to this passage is our most direct and indubitable source of data about the nature of time.
3. Therefore, the true ontology of time must incorporate dynamic becoming (Presentism or GBU). Static eternalism (Block Universe) or shrinking/retrograde models must be rejected because they fail to account for the manifest image of temporal flow.

#### The Undermining of the Argument
The demonstration of phenomenological indistinguishability across GBU, DBU, and BBU completely subverts this evidential inference. If a subjective experience of a dynamic "now," an accumulating past, and an open future can be generated with equal fidelity by:
*   An ontologically growing universe (GBU),
*   An ontologically diminishing universe (DBU),
*   A retrogradely building universe (BBU),
*   (And arguably, even by sophisticated eternalist block models equipped with temporal indexical mechanisms like standard A-theories or B-theoretic subjective projection),

...then the phenomenology of temporal flow is **underdetermined** by subjective experience. 

Underdetermination is fatal to the evidential weight of the Phenomenological Argument. When multiple, mutually exclusive ontological structures yield the exact same observational (in this case, introspective) output, introspective experience ceases to be a discriminator between those hypotheses. Just as the Ptolemaic and Copernican systems could (for a time) account for the same visual phenomena of planetary motion, requiring external theoretical constraints to break the tie, human consciousness of "the now" cannot adjudicate between GBU, DBU, and BBU.

To insist that our feeling of the "now" proves the Growing Block or Presentism is to commit a classic fallacy of affirming the consequent:
*   *Premise:* If GBU is true, we will experience temporal flow.
*   *Observation:* We experience temporal flow.
*   *Conclusion:* Therefore, GBU is true.

The validity of this inference is shattered the moment we introduce the conditional:
*   *Premise:* If DBU (or BBU) is true, we will *also* experience temporal flow.

Consequently, common-sense human experience lends **zero** special evidential weight to traditional presentist or growing-block ontologies over their heterodox alternatives. The manifest image of time is entirely neutral regarding the macro-ontological direction of temporal becoming.

### VII. Conclusion: The Limits of Introspective Metaphysics

The exploration of Diminishing Block and Backwards Block theories forces a sobering realization upon philosophers of time: our subjective intimacy with the temporal stream does not grant us privileged access to the fundamental architecture of reality. 

We have established that:
1. **Functional Equivalence:** The neuro-cognitive mechanics of memory hierarchies, informational gradients, and the psychological scaling of importance can generate a subjective experience of temporal flow that is completely indistinguishable whether the universe is growing, shrinking, or building in reverse.
2. **Coherence:** While Dummett’s critique successfully dismantles incoherent notions of local retrocausal agency, it fails to invalidate global retrograde ontologies like the Backwards Block, provided local computational and thermodynamic consistency is maintained.
3. **Evidential Bankruptcy:** The psychological indistinguishability of these temporal models utterly subverts the evidential weight that common-sense human experience is traditionally thought to lend to Presentism and the Growing Block theory.

Ultimately, the feeling of the "now" is not a metaphysical compass pointing toward the ontological edge of the universe. It is a localized, adaptive illusion of information processing—a computational interface designed to navigate entropy gradients, regardless of whether those gradients are carved out of an expanding reality, a diminishing reserve, or a retrograde wave originating from the end of time. Metaphysics must abandon the pretense that introspection can solve the riddles of temporal becoming, and instead turn to the austere constraints of physics, logic, and global coherence to map the true shape of the block.
