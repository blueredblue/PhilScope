### The Illusion of the Instant: Kripke, Temporal Rigidity, and the Failure of Stage-Theoretic Identity

The persistence of material objects through time is a cornerstone of our common-sense ontology. We speak of the same pencil, Penny, being sharpened yesterday, used today, and discarded tomorrow, assuming a single entity that endures across these changes. Yet, this intuitive picture faces profound metaphysical challenges, particularly when we consider the nature of time, change, and identity. One radical response to these challenges is the claim that material objects do not endure at all. Instead, they exist only for single instants, and what we call their “identity through time” is nothing more than an abstract sequence of distinct, instantaneous physical states.
