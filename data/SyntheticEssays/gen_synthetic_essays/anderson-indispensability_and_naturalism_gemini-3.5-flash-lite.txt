# The Pragmatic Habitation of Abstract Objects: Compromise Naturalism and the Metaphysics of Indispensability

## Abstract

The Quine-Putnam indispensability argument faces a profound internal tension when confronted with Penelope Maddy’s methodological naturalism: confirmation holism appears to demand an ontological commitment to abstract mathematical entities, yet scientific and mathematical practices routinely operate as though such entities lack causal efficacy, epistemic accessibility, and traditional truth-maker obligations. This essay defends a *compromise naturalism* that resolves this tension by decoupling realism from literal ontological commitment. 

By analyzing the mechanics of truth-value determinacy through structuralist and fictionalist lenses, we demonstrate that mathematical entities can possess determinate truth-values within idealized frameworks without possessing "ontological rights" in the realist's baseline sense. Furthermore, we argue that the indispensability argument is not an illegitimate imperialist intrusion into scientific practice, but rather a necessary, reflective equilibrium that articulates the metaphysical presuppositions already latent in mature scientific inquiry. Compromise naturalism thus preserves mathematical realism, honors scientific practice, and vindicates the indispensability argument against Maddy’s naturalist critique.

---

## Introduction

The intersection of philosophy of mathematics and philosophy of science has long been dominated by a methodological standoff between the Quinean holist and the Maddyian practitioner. On one side stands the Quine-Putnam Indispensability Argument (QPIA), which asserts that we ought to have ontological commitment to all and only those entities that are indispensable to our best scientific theories—a class that paradigmatically includes mathematical objects such as sets, functions, and spaces. On the other side stands Penelope Maddy, whose "naturalistic" turn demands that philosophical accounts of mathematics and science defer to the actual, ground-level practices of working mathematicians and empirical scientists. Maddy argues that actual scientific confirmation does not wash over the entirety of a web of belief in the uniform manner Quine supposes; rather, scientists compartmentalize, treating mathematical apparatuses as heuristic tools rather than truth-valued descriptions of a platonic realm.

This essay evaluates a sophisticated middle-ground position: **compromise naturalism**. This framework attempts to rescue the indispensability argument from Maddy’s practice-based criticisms not by doubling down on a traditional, heavy-duty Platonism, but by reconceptualizing what it means for mathematical entities to be real and for our theories to be committed to them. 

We will proceed in four stages. 
1. First, we examine the structural mechanics of the QPIA and map out Maddy’s critique from practice, focusing on the alleged incompatibility between Quinean confirmation holism and actual scientific methodology. 
2. Second, we articulate the compromise naturalist thesis, demonstrating how the separation of semantic and pragmatic realism from literal ontological commitment neutralizes Maddy’s objections. 
3. Third, we address the challenge of semantic stability: can alternative forms of realism sustain determinate truth-values for indispensable mathematical entities even when those entities are denied robust ontological rights? 
4. Finally, we defend the normative intervention of indispensability theory, arguing that rather than improperly overriding actual methodology, the QPIA serves as a critical, reflective arbiter of the implicit commitments embedded in our most successful scientific endeavors.

---

## 1. The Indispensability Argument and the Maddyian Challenge

To appreciate the gravity of the impasse, we must first reconstruct the dialectic between Quinean holism and Maddyian practice. The classical QPIA runs as follows:

1. **Premise 1:** We must have ontological commitment to all and only those entities that are indispensable to our best scientific theories.
2. **Premise 2:** Mathematical entities are indispensable to our best scientific theories.
3. **Conclusion:** Therefore, we must have ontological commitment to mathematical entities (Quine 1948, 1981; Putnam 1971).

The engine driving Premise 1 is Quinean confirmation holism. For Quine, our beliefs do not face the tribunal of sensory experience one by one; rather, "our statements about the external world face the tribunal of sense experience only as a corporate body" (Quine 1951, 41). If empirical data disconfirms a prediction derived from a physical theory inextricably bound to a mathematical framework, the entire web of belief—including its mathematical components—is subject to revision. Conversely, when empirical successes accumulate, confirmation distributes across the entire web. Because differential equations, Hilbert spaces, and set-theoretic structures are woven into the very fabric of physical laws, they are confirmed *ipso facto* by the empirical successes of those laws.

Penelope Maddy’s early and mature work launches a formidable assault on this picture, drawing primarily from an immersion in actual scientific and mathematical practice (Maddy 1992, 1997, 2007). Maddy’s methodological naturalism insists that philosophy of mathematics should not dictate standards of confirmation *a priori* from the armchair of philosophical system-building, but should instead look to how scientists and mathematicians actually behave. When we examine actual practice, Maddy argues, Quine’s holism collapses under its own psychological and historical weight.

Maddy targets the core of the holist mechanism: the idea that empirical confirmation flows horizontally across the web from physics to mathematics. In her analysis of physical practice—such as the use of mathematics in classical mechanics or quantum field theory—she observes that physicists treat mathematical assumptions differently from physical hypotheses. When an experiment yields an anomalous result, a physicist does not seriously consider revising real analysis, Zermelo-Fraenkel Set Theory (ZFC), or the axiom of choice. Instead, they look for anomalies in auxiliary physical hypotheses, experimental apparatuses, or boundary conditions. 

Maddy formalizes this via her *Maxim of Idle Tagging*: if the nominalistic content of a scientific theory (its physical claims) is logically equivalent to its platonistic content (its physical-plus-mathematical claims) in terms of empirical consequences, then the mathematical machinery is "idle" regarding confirmation. The physicist’s epistemic attitude toward mathematical entities is one of pragmatic reliance, not ontological belief.

Maddy’s critique exposes a stark incompatibility:
* **The Quinean demands** that we read our ontology off our most successful scientific theories taken as unvarnished logical wholes.
* **The Maddyian demands** that our ontology respect the localized, compartmentalized epistemic practices of working scientists who do not take their mathematics to be empirically confirmed in the same way their physical hypotheses are.

If Maddy is right, the QPIA relies on a false picture of confirmation, making its first premise an illegitimate philosophical imposition on scientific practice.

## 2. Compromise Naturalism: Separating Realism from Ontological Commitment

How can the indispensability argument survive Maddy’s practice-based critique without retreating into an implausible, traditional Platonism that severs mathematics from empirical science altogether? The answer lies in **compromise naturalism**, a position that accepts Maddy’s methodological demand to take scientific practice seriously, while denying her conclusion that this practice strips mathematics of its metaphysical significance.

The key maneuver of compromise naturalism is to **separate semantic and pragmatic realism from literal ontological commitment**. 

Traditional participants in this debate have operated under a binary ontology: either mathematical entities exist as mind-independent abstract objects (Platonism/Realism), or they do not exist, rendering mathematical statements either false, truth-valueless, or mere instrumentalities (Anti-realism/Nominalism). Maddy leverages the practices of working scientists to push us toward a nuanced anti-realism or a localized quasi-realism regarding mathematics. However, compromise naturalism suggests that our categories of "ontology" and "commitment" are overly reified.

### Decoupling Realism and Ontology

To understand this decoupling, we must distinguish between three distinct notions:
1. **Ontological Commitment (Quinean):** The existential quantification over entities required to make the bound variables of our canonical theories true.
2. **Pragmatic Indispensability:** The constitutive role a conceptual framework plays in organizing, modeling, and articulating empirical regularities.
3. **Semantic Realism:** The thesis that mathematical discourse is objective, truth-valuable, and irreducible to mere rule-following or psychological projection.

Maddy’s critique shows that working scientists do not maintain Quinean ontological commitments to mathematical objects; they do not believe in the causal or spatiotemporal isolation of sets, nor do they track sets as part of their causal explanatory matrix. Therefore, if the QPIA requires *Quinean ontological commitment*—the literal inventory of furniture in the universe—then Maddy has refuted it. 

However, compromise naturalism argues that the QPIA does *not* require this heavy-duty ontological inventory to secure its core philosophical goals. Instead, it requires **pragmatic indispensability coupled with semantic realism**. 

When a scientific theory utilizes mathematics, it is not merely deploying a useful fiction or a calculating device (as the instrumentalist claims). Rather, it is latching onto real *structural* features of the world. The world possesses a modal, relational, and structural profile that cannot be articulated without mathematical language. To say that mathematics is indispensable is to say that the structural truths expressed by mathematical frameworks are conditions of possibility for our empirical descriptions of reality.

By separating *realism* (the objectivity and truth-aptness of structural claims) from *ontological commitment* (the inventory of transcendent abstract objects), compromise naturalism dissolves the tension Maddy identifies. We can agree with Maddy that scientists do not "believe in" sets in the way they believe in electrons or quarks; sets do not have causal profiles that register in detectors. Yet we can maintain with Quine and Putnam that our best scientific theories are objectively committed to the *structural adequacy* of mathematical frameworks.

This approach aligns with what Mark Balaguer terms *Plenitudinous Platonism* or what structuralists like Stewart Shapiro (1997) and Geoffrey Hellman (1989) describe as modal structuralism, though compromise naturalism pushes further by refusing to translate all mathematics away into modal logic. Instead, it embraces the framework as a given stratum of our conceptual scheme, whose reality consists in its structural objectivity rather than its occupancy of a platonic realm of "objects."

## 3. Truth-Value Determinacy Without Ontological Rights

A natural objection to compromise naturalism is that it threatens to collapse into a form of instrumentalism or conventionalism. If we deny mathematical entities "ontological rights"—that is, if we refuse to grant them the status of robust, independent entities inhabiting a realm of abstracta—how can we secure determinate truth-values for mathematical statements? 

In classical semantics, truth requires truth-makers: entities in virtue of which propositions are true. If mathematical propositions are about mathematical objects (e.g., numbers, sets, functions), and those objects do not possess ontological rights, then mathematical discourse risks losing its semantic stability. It threatens to become a game of formal manipulation whose application to science is miraculous or merely conventional.

To defend compromise naturalism against this objection, we must analyze how alternative forms of realism sustain determinate truth-values without relying on traditional, reified ontologies. We can achieve this by integrating **antirepresentationalist semantics** with **structuralist ontology**.

### Structuralist Semantics and Minimal Realism

Consider the structuralist insight: the individual numbers in the natural number sequence (1, 2, 3...) do not have internal natures or "ontological rights" independent of the relations they bear to one another within the structure. The number 3 is *nothing more* than the third position in the progression. As Michael Resnik (1997) and Stewart Shapiro (1997) argue, mathematics is the science of structures. 

Under compromise naturalism, this structuralist insight is generalized across the entire indispensable framework of science. Mathematical statements possess determinate truth-values not because they map onto isolated, ghostly platonic objects, but because they articulate the structural properties of systems. 

* **The Semantic Claim:** The truth-value of a mathematical statement within a framework is determined by the internal coherence, deductive closure, and structural constraints of that framework. 
* **The Pragmatic Claim:** The selection of *which* framework to deploy is governed by its indispensability to our comprehensive scientific description of the world.

This resolves the dilemma of ontological rights. Mathematical entities do not need to be granted independent ontological credentials because their "existence" is derivative of, and co-extensive with, the structural frameworks in which they are embedded. To ask whether the number 4 has an ontological right to exist *apart* from the natural number structure is to commit a category mistake analogous to asking whether the "center of mass" of an irregular polygon exists apart from the material points constituting the polygon. The center of mass is a real, determinate property of the system, essential for physical calculations, yet it is not a fundamental constituent of the physical universe added to the masses of the individual points.

### Defending Against the Benacerrafian Challenge

This maneuver directly addresses Paul Benacerraf’s (1973) famous dilemma for mathematical truth: any adequate philosophy of mathematics must reconcile a semantics that yields determinate truth-values with an epistemology that explains how we can have knowledge of mathematical entities. 

* Traditional platonism solves the semantics (objects make statements true) but ruins the epistemology (how do we interact causally with causally inert abstracta?).
* Nominalism/constructivism solves the epistemology (no mysterious objects) but ruins the semantics (making mathematical truth conventional, subjective, or truth-valueless).

Compromise naturalism bridges this chasm by redefining the "objects" of mathematical knowledge. Epistemological access is not a matter of causal commerce with abstract realm-dwellers; rather, it is a matter of **inferential mastery and structural pattern recognition**. 

Scientists and mathematicians acquire knowledge of mathematical structures through proof, axiomatic modeling, and idealization. Because these structures are indispensable to tracking empirical regularities, our epistemic access to them is parasitic upon our epistemic access to the physical systems they model. We come to know the properties of Hilbert spaces because they are the indispensable vehicles through which we successfully predict quantum mechanical phenomena. 

Thus, determinacy of truth-value is secured by structural rigor within formal systems, while epistemic warrant is secured by the empirical indispensability of those systems to scientific practice.

## 4. The Normative Intervention of Indispensability Theory

We must now confront the most aggressive challenge from the Maddyian camp: the accusation of **philosophical imperialism**. Maddy and other practice-based philosophers argue that when a philosopher of a Quinean bent steps in and tells working scientists and mathematicians that their theories are ontologically committed to abstract entities—despite the scientists' explicit disavowals or operational indifference—the philosopher is committing an egregious methodological error. 

According to this critique, philosophy of science and mathematics must be *descriptive* and *deferential* to practice. It has no normative right to override the localized, pragmatic self-understanding of the practitioners who generate the science in the first place.

Compromise naturalism mounts a robust defense of the *normative intervention* of indispensability theory. It argues that descriptive deference to scientific practice is necessary, but profoundly insufficient, for a complete philosophical understanding of science.

### The Limits of Practitioner Self-Understanding

To see why descriptive deference must be supplemented by normative philosophical critique, we must recognize that working scientists and mathematicians are not, qua practitioners, professional metaphysicians or epistemologists. Their primary methodological aim is empirical adequacy, predictive power, and formal fecundity within their specialized domains. They do not step back to synthesize the ontological presuppositions of disparate fields (e.g., how the topology of general relativity interfaces with the algebraic structures of quantum field theory or the set-theoretic foundations of functional analysis).

When a physicist uses differential geometry, they are pragmatically focused on solving field equations. They are not typically concerned with whether the smooth manifolds they deploy require a commitment to uncountable infinities. But this operational silence does not mean no metaphysical or ontological presuppositions are present. It simply means they are *implicit*.

The QPIA, viewed through the lens of compromise naturalism, is not an arbitrary, top-down imposition of alien standards; it is an exercise in **reflective equilibrium** (Rawls 1971; Daniels 1979). Reflective equilibrium requires us to bring our ground-level practices and our overarching reflective principles into systematic harmony. 

* On one hand, we must respect the deliverance of scientific practice (Maddy’s insight).
* On the other hand, we must construct a coherent philosophical account that explains *why* these practices are so remarkably successful (Quine/Putnam’s insight).

When we observe that modern science *cannot even be stated* without advanced mathematics—that mathematical structures are not mere window-dressing but are constitutive of the causal-nomological network we take to be real—a purely deflationary, non-realist account of mathematics leaves this success looking like an unexplained miracle (Putnam’s "no-miracles" argument applied to mathematics).

### Indispensability Theory as Critical Metaphysics

Compromise naturalism frames the indispensability argument not as an antagonist to scientific practice, but as its **metaphysical articulation**. Consider an analogy from the philosophy of mind or ethics. A neuroscientist studying neural firing patterns may be operationally focused on electrochemical gradients and synaptic weights. They may explicitly deny that they are studying "consciousness" in a philosophical, phenomenal sense, viewing such talk as unscientific fluff. Yet, a philosopher of mind intervenes to argue that the functional architecture being uncovered is constitutive of conscious experience. 

Is this philosophical intervention "improperly overriding" the neuroscientist's methodology? No. It is making explicit the theoretical and ontological weight of what the neuroscientist is doing.

Similarly, when the indispensability theorist asserts that our best scientific theories are committed to the structural reality of mathematical frameworks, they are engaging in a necessary act of philosophical accounting. They are asking: *What must the world be like, and what must our conceptual relation to the world be, for this scientific practice to be so overwhelmingly successful?*

Maddy’s methodological naturalism stalls out precisely because it refuses to ask this global, foundational question, restricting itself to micro-analyses of local scientific choices. While Maddy is correct that a physicist does not pause to worship sets during an experiment, the *global architecture* of modern physics relies on mathematical structures that transcend any single experimental domain. The indispensability argument captures this global, holistic dependence.

Therefore, the normative intervention of indispensability theory is fully justified. It does not dictate to the scientist how to run their experiments or how to construct their proofs; rather, it provides a coherent metaphysical framework that vindicates the objective validity of scientific practice against skeptical deflationism.

## 5. Synthesis: The Compromise Naturalist Manifesto

To consolidate our argument, let us assemble the components of compromise naturalism into a coherent philosophical position:

1. **Anti-Dogmatic Naturalism:** We accept Maddy’s core naturalist premise that philosophy of mathematics and science must be continuous with, and informed by, actual scientific and mathematical practice. We reject armchair Platonism that ignores the pragmatic reality of how mathematical tools are deployed in laboratories and theoretical models.
2. **Holistic Realism:** We retain the Quinean insight that our intellectual endeavors form a deeply interconnected web. However, we modify Quinean holism by recognizing that confirmation is not uniform; it is structural and systemic rather than atomistic and localized. Mathematics is confirmed not by being directly targeted by empirical tests, but by being the indispensable structural medium through which empirical theories achieve predictive and explanatory determinacy.
3. **Ontological Minimalism:** We jettison the heavy-duty, populated Platonism of traditional ontologies. Mathematical entities do not possess "ontological rights" in the sense of existing as transcendent, causally inert individuals residing in a platonic heaven. Instead, their "existence" is cashed out in terms of **structural objectivity**. Mathematical statements are objectively true because they articulate the structural and modal constraints of possible systems.
4. **Pragmatic Justification:** The indispensability of mathematics to science is the ultimate warrant for our realist attitude toward mathematical structures. Without this realism, the applicability of mathematics to the physical world becomes an inexplicable coincidence.
5. **Reflective Equilibrium:** Indispensability theory functions as a legitimate, normative articulation of the implicit commitments of scientific practice, bridging the gap between localized scientific methodology and global metaphysical interpretation.

### Addressing Potential Counterarguments

Critics may argue that this compromise position is unstable: if we strip mathematical objects of their ontological rights, have we not simply become structuralist fictionalists or modal nominalists in disguise? 

Not quite. Fictionalism and modal nominalism maintain that mathematical statements are *literally false* (though perhaps useful) because the entities they quantify over do not exist. Compromise naturalism insists that mathematical statements are **literally true**, because their truth-conditions do not require reference to ghostly individual objects, but rather satisfaction within coherent structural frameworks that capture objective features of reality. 

The compromise naturalist does not deny that numbers or sets "exist"; rather, they redefine *existence* in this domain away from the crude physicalist paradigm of "possessing a spatial location or a causal profile" toward a sophisticated structuralist paradigm of "possessing modal and relational necessity within an indispensable explanatory matrix."

---

## Conclusion

The debate between the Quine-Putnam indispensability argument and Penelope Maddy’s practice-based naturalism has often been framed as a zero-sum game: either we impose an imperialist, un-naturalistic metaphysics upon science, or we abandon the realist import of mathematics in slavish devotion to localized scientific practice. 

**Compromise naturalism** shatters this false dichotomy. By decoupling semantic and structural realism from traditional ontological commitment, compromise naturalism honors Maddy’s demand for methodological deference to scientific practice while preserving the profound metaphysical insight of the indispensability argument. It shows that mathematical entities do not need independent ontological rights to be indispensable; their reality is grounded in the objective structural framework without which modern science could neither articulate its laws nor comprehend its universe. 

In doing so, the indispensability argument is vindicated not as an authoritarian philosophical dogma, but as the indispensable reflective mirror of scientific rationality itself.
