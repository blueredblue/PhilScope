The concept of aesthetic emergence offers a profound solution to the Whiteheadian problem of temporal perishing, where the fleeting nature of reality threatens to undermine the coherence and meaning of our experiences. By integrating novelty and ritual, aesthetic emergence provides a framework for understanding how artistic expression can transcend the limitations of time and space, offering a redemptive vision that reconciles the subjective and objective aspects of human experience. This essay will explore the tension between subjective resonance and objective appraisal, and examine how hybrid prehensions and poetic insights enable artistic expression to function as a secular and theological redemption from the perpetual flux of actual entities.

In Whitehead's process philosophy, the universe is composed of actual entities, which are momentary, discrete, and perpetually perishing. This fundamental ontology raises the question of how we can account for the endurance and coherence of our experiences, given the transience of the actual entities that comprise them. Whitehead himself addressed this issue through the concept of "societies," which refers to the ways in which actual entities can be grouped and related to form more complex, enduring structures. However, this solution still leaves open the question of how we can reconcile the subjective, internal experience of these societies with the objective, external world that they inhabit.

Aesthetic emergence offers a unique perspective on this problem, by highlighting the role of artistic expression in integrating novelty and ritual. On one hand, novelty refers to the introduction of new, unforeseen elements into a system, which can disrupt and transform existing patterns and structures. On the other hand, ritual refers to the repetition and reenactment of established practices and traditions, which can provide a sense of continuity and coherence. By combining these two elements, aesthetic emergence creates a dynamic, iterative process that allows artistic expression to evolve and adapt over time, while still maintaining a connection to the past.

This integration of novelty and ritual is particularly evident in the work of artists who engage with traditional forms and techniques, while also introducing new and innovative elements. For example, a musician who combines elements of jazz and classical music to create a unique sound can be seen as exemplifying aesthetic emergence, as they are introducing novelty into a established ritualistic framework. Similarly, a poet who draws on traditional forms and themes, while also experimenting with new language and imagery, can be seen as embodying the tension between subjective resonance and objective appraisal that is at the heart of aesthetic emergence.

This tension arises from the fact that artistic expression is both a deeply personal, subjective experience, and a public, objective phenomenon. On one hand, the creation and appreciation of art are rooted in the individual's internal experience, and are shaped by their unique perspective, emotions, and values. On the other hand, art is also a social and cultural phenomenon, which is subject to external evaluation, criticism, and interpretation. This tension between subjective resonance and objective appraisal is a fundamental challenge for aesthetic emergence, as it must reconcile the internal, personal experience of art with the external, public nature of artistic expression.

Hybrid prehensions offer a key solution to this problem, by allowing artistic expression to incorporate and integrate multiple perspectives, modes, and levels of experience. In Whitehead's philosophy, prehensions refer to the basic units of experience, which are the fundamental building blocks of reality. Hybrid prehensions, then, refer to the combination of different types of prehensions, such as physical, emotional, and conceptual, to create a new, integrated whole. By allowing artistic expression to incorporate and synthesize multiple modes of experience, hybrid prehensions enable the creation of complex, nuanced, and multifaceted works of art that can resonate with a wide range of audiences and perspectives.

Poetic insights play a crucial role in facilitating this process, by providing a unique perspective on the world that is both personal and universal. Poetry, in particular, has a long history of exploring the human condition, and of expressing the deepest and most profound aspects of human experience. Through the use of imagery, metaphor, and symbolism, poetry can create a rich, textured, and highly nuanced language that is capable of conveying complex emotions, ideas, and experiences. By tapping into this poetic tradition, artistic expression can gain access to a deep well of cultural and historical knowledge, and can draw on a wide range of symbolic and metaphorical resources to create new and innovative works.

The integration of novelty and ritual, facilitated by hybrid prehensions and poetic insights, allows artistic expression to function as a secular and theological redemption from the perpetual flux of actual entities. In a secular sense, artistic expression can provide a sense of meaning, purpose, and coherence in a world that is often fragmented and disjointed. By creating complex, nuanced, and multifaceted works of art, artists can offer a vision of the world that is both beautiful and meaningful, and that can inspire and uplift audiences. In a theological sense, artistic expression can provide a connection to the divine, or to a higher power, that transcends the limitations of time and space. By tapping into the deeper, more profound aspects of human experience, artistic expression can offer a glimpse of the eternal, the infinite, and the absolute, and can provide a sense of redemption and salvation from the flux and uncertainty of the world.

This redemptive vision is particularly evident in the work of artists who engage with spiritual or religious themes, such as the use of sacred imagery, symbolism, or ritual in their work. For example, a painter who creates works that depict spiritual or mystical experiences can be seen as embodying the tension between subjective resonance and objective appraisal, as they are creating a public, objective work of art that is rooted in their own personal, subjective experience. Similarly, a musician who composes music that is inspired by spiritual or religious themes can be seen as exemplifying the integration of novelty and ritual, as they are introducing new and innovative elements into a established ritualistic framework.
